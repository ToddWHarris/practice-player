      Welcome

      Practice Player is a simple music player for practicing along with songs. You can loop a hard part and say "again" to hear it once more.

      • Your songs, playlists and settings stay on this device. Nothing is uploaded and there is no account.
      • Each person who installs the app on their own phone has their own separate copy.
      • The app has seven tabs: Library, Playlists, Metronome, Tuner, Level, Settings and Help. On a narrow phone, swipe the tab bar sideways if a tab is off the screen.

      Share this app

      • Tap Share this app here or near the top of Settings to send the app link to family and friends by Messages, Mail or any other app.
      • Only the link to the app is shared. Your songs, playlists and settings are never shared. Each person imports their own music.
      • If your phone cannot open the share sheet, the link is copied so you can paste it. If copying does not work either, the link is shown so you can select and copy it.

      Getting MP3s onto your phone

      The app imports MP3 files that you can see in the Files app (on Android, in your phone's files). It cannot use songs from Apple Music or the Music app.

      Ways to get songs from a computer to an iPhone:

      • iCloud Drive: on the computer, copy your song folder into iCloud Drive (Finder on a Mac, iCloud for Windows, or icloud.com in a web browser). Wait for it to upload. On the iPhone, open Files, then iCloud Drive. Tip: press and hold the folder and tap Download Now so the songs are on the phone before you import.
      • USB drive: copy the songs onto a USB stick or memory card and plug it into the iPhone (use an adapter if needed). It shows up in Files under Locations.
      • AirDrop from a Mac, or a cloud app like Google Drive, Dropbox or OneDrive, also works. Anything that shows up in Files can be imported.
      • If you like, move the folder into Files, On My iPhone, so it is kept on the phone. This is optional. You can import straight from iCloud Drive or a USB drive.

      On Android, copy the songs with a USB cable into the phone's Music or Download folder, or use Google Drive.

      Importing songs

      Go to Settings, then Your music.

      • Import folder copies every MP3 in the folder you pick, and in all of its subfolders, into the app. The folder layout is kept. Big folders can take a minute. Wait for the "Imported" message.
      • Import files lets you pick one or more songs. Select several at once to save time. On Android, use this if Import folder does not work.
      • Imports are copies kept in the app's own private storage. You will not see them in the Files app, and nothing is uploaded.
      • After importing, it is OK to delete the originals from your phone to save space. Keeping a backup on your computer or in iCloud is a good idea. Until you delete them, your songs use about double the space.
      • You can import more at any time. Importing the same song again (same name, folder and size) replaces it, so you do not get duplicates.
      • To hide one song, swipe it to the left in the Library and tap Remove. It goes to the Removed folder and can be restored. To erase it for good, open Removed, swipe it left and tap Delete forever.
      • Deleting the app from your Home Screen, or clearing Safari website data, erases the app's copies. You would need to import again.

Send shares the song file (see Sending songs to someone). Remove moves it to the Removed folder. Swipe right or tap the song to close them without changes. Only one song is open at a time.
      • Removed appears last in the Library, with a count, when it holds songs. Removed songs are hidden from their folder and are skipped when you play a folder or playlist. They stay in your playlists, shown in grey. Inside Removed, removed folders are kept together and show their original path, and each song shows the folder it came from. Swipe a folder or song left and tap Restore to put it back, or Delete forever to erase it (you are asked to confirm). You can also open a removed folder and restore or delete single songs. Importing the same file again also restores it.

      • Next (⏭) and Back (⏮) follow your current queue: the playlist or folder you started from. The card shows "Playing from" so you always know which one.

      • Back goes to the start of the song if you are more than 3 seconds in. Tap it again to go to the previous song.

      • Shuffle plays your current queue in random order, starting with the song that is playing. Turn it off to go back to the normal order.
      • Repeat changes each time you tap it: off, then playlist (the whole queue starts over at the end), then one song (the same song plays again and again), then back to off.

      A-B loop and voice

      1. Tap A-B loop under the player to open it.
      2. Play the song. Tap Set A where the part starts and Set B where it ends. Use A −0.5, A +0.5, B −0.5 and B +0.5 to fine tune by half a second. The seek bar shows the loop.
      3. Tap Start loop. It plays from A and pauses at B.
      4. At B the app shows "Listening". Say go, again, repeat or yes to play from A again. Say stop to end the loop.
      5. The big Again button always works too. To end the loop, tap Stop loop.

      For voice to work

      • Auto repeat must be unchecked.
      • Turn on Dictation: iPhone Settings, General, Keyboard, Enable Dictation.
      • The first time you tap Start loop, iPhone asks to use the microphone. Tap Allow.
      • Keep the app open on screen. An internet connection helps.
      • If listening stops, a Tap to listen button appears. Tap it to turn the mic back on.

      Auto repeat (no voice)

      • Check Auto repeat and set the gap in seconds. After each pass the app waits that long, then plays from A again by itself. The mic is not used.
      • Clear removes A and B. Starting a different song also clears A and B. Opening Settings ends the loop but keeps A and B.

      • Pick a time signature. Beat one gets a higher click and a different flash color.

      • Save keeps the tempo and time signature under a name. Pick it from the Saved beats list to load it again, or tap Delete. Saved beats go into your Backup.
      • Tap Mic off to turn voice on. Then say "start", "stop", or a tempo like "one twenty", "ninety" or "one hundred twenty". It only listens while the app is open on screen and the Metronome tab is showing.
      • The metronome stops when you leave the tab or play a song. On iPhone the silent switch can mute the clicks, so flip it off if you hear nothing.

      Loop editor

      • Once A and B are set, tap Loop editor in the A-B section for a big, zoomed in view of your loop.
      • Drag the A and B handles with your finger. They move in whole seconds (1 s steps). A always stays at least 1 second before B.
      • The view also shows some of the song before and after the loop, 10 seconds each by default. Change this in Settings: tap Loop and practice settings and set Before and After, then tap Done. When you let go of a handle, the view adjusts so you can drag further. Near the start of a song you can drag A all the way to 0:00.

      Tuner

      • Open the Tuner tab. Tap Start to use the microphone. Tap Stop, or leave the tab, to turn the mic off.
      • You see cents, a framed meter, and the frequency in Hz. The cents marks stay still, every 10 cents, with zero in the center. A green zone moves left when you are flat and right when you are sharp. Note names slide on the lower band with the pitch. Flat moves toward the lower note. Sharp moves toward the higher note. On a piano, with the phone sideways, the note on the left is the one to tune. It stays put. Other instruments do not show that side note, and portrait does not either.
      • A= sets the reference from 430 to 450 Hz (default 440). Your choice is saved on this device.
      • Instrument picks how notes are shown. Auto / Chromatic and Piano show any note. Guitar and Bass guitar highlight open strings (and Bass can add a low B0 for 5-string). Trumpet (B flat) shows written pitch, a major second above concert. Euphonium defaults to concert pitch (bass clef); you can switch to treble clef B flat, written a major 9th above concert.
      • Piano has a Target menu: Stretch (typical) uses a typical Railsback curve (bass a little flat, treble a little sharp, A4 at 0), Equal temperament uses plain equal temperament, or pick a saved profile from this piano. The screen shows the target offset, for example Target +12 cents (stretch). Real piano tuners tailor the curve to each piano, so the typical stretch is a guide and not a replacement for a professional tuner.
      • Sample this piano walks through the 88 keys from A0 to C8. Play each key and hold it until the reading is steady; the app stores that offset from equal temperament. Tap Skip key to leave a gap (gaps are filled by interpolating the sampled curve). Tap Save profile to name it (for example Church piano). The date and A reference are stored with it. You can rename, delete, or re-sample one note later. Profiles are saved on this device and go into your Backup.
      • The mic only runs while the Tuner tab is open and Start is on. Playing a song, opening Level, or leaving the tab stops it.
      • Guided piano tune opens a landscape screen. Pick Concert grand (9 ft), Baby grand, Studio upright, Spinet, Typical, or a saved profile. The pre-scan plays no tone and speaks nothing. It measures every note from A0 to C8, however sharp or flat, then shows Got it and Next. The mic stays on. Unreadable notes can be retried. Tap OK on the Tuning mode card before any string is tuned. Each string is tuned on its own, and the next note waits until you tap Done. A new piano asks you to pick the style and the string splits. Saved pianos can be loaded later. Later tuning steps still play the note, and a key turns green within about 2 cents. Back and Steps repeat any step. Tap Help on that screen for the hammer, the mutes, and how to turn a pin. Progress is saved on this device and included in Backup.

      Piano stretch curves

      Smaller pianos stretch more. Their strings are shorter, so they are stiffer. A stiff string rings a little sharp in its upper partials. That is called inharmonicity. Tuners stretch the scale so those partials line up. Bass notes sit flat of equal temperament. Treble notes sit sharp. A spinet stretches more than a studio upright. A studio upright stretches more than a baby grand. A baby grand stretches more than a 9 ft concert grand.

      The chart is cents versus the note, from A0 to C8. The zero line is equal temperament. A4 is the vertical line at 0 cents. Flat is below the line. Sharp is above it.

      Typical uses the Fernhart Labs grand standard anchor points already in this app (A0, C1, C2, C3, A3, C4, A4, C5, C6, C7, and C8). Those points are sourced. The app fills the notes between them. Concert grand (9 ft), Baby grand, Studio upright, and Spinet are calculated from string stiffness. They are derived, not a measurement of one piano, and not copied from another tuner. Real tuners fit each piano. Use the chart as a starting map.

      Piano tuning tools

      This app tells you when a string is sharp or flat. The tools below are what you use on the piano itself.

      What you need

      Tuning hammer (also called a tuning lever)
      A handle with a star tip that fits over the tuning pin. Most home pianos use a size 2 tip. This is the tool that turns the pin to raise or lower the pitch. Use a tip that fits snugly. A loose tip can slip and damage the pin.

      Mute strip (felt temperament strip)
      A long strip of felt you weave between the strings in the middle of the piano. It quiets the outer strings of each three-string note so only the center string speaks. That lets you set the middle of the piano one string at a time.

      Rubber mutes (wedges)
      Small rubber wedges, often with a wire handle. Push one between strings to quiet the ones you are not tuning. You only want to hear the string you are turning.

      Felt mutes (wedges)
      Same idea as rubber mutes, but made of felt. Many people like felt because it holds well and mutes cleanly, especially on grands and bass notes.

      Papp's mutes (mainly for uprights)
      Small mutes for tight spots in the high treble of an upright, where a long strip or big wedge will not fit. Optional on many grands.

      Tuning fork
      Optional. This app already gives you the target pitch. Keep a fork only if you want a backup you can hear.

      A simple starter kit is usually a decent hammer with a size 2 tip, a felt mute strip, and several rubber or felt wedges. Add Papp's mutes if you tune uprights a lot.

      How to use the tuning hammer

      1. Mute so only one string speaks for the note you are on.
      2. Seat the tip fully on that string's tuning pin.
      3. Hold the handle so it points roughly along the string (think of a clock near 12:00 to 1:30). That reduces unwanted bending of the pin.
      4. Turn with small moves. Feel the pin actually turn in the wood, not just twist at the top.
      5. Raise the pitch up to the green target when you can. Coming up from a little flat is often more stable than easing down from sharp.
      6. Play a firm test blow and watch the needle. If it drifts, nudge again and retest.
      7. Do not lean hard on the pin to fake the pitch. Heavy lean can bend the pin and the note will not hold.

      Some pins like a smooth pull. Tight pins often like tiny controlled jerks (sometimes called notch tuning). Adapt to what that pin needs. If a pin will not move without force, stop and get help rather than forcing it.

      Why mutes matter with this app

      The screen guides one string at a time and says what it is listening for. A single note means pitch only. A pair of notes means beats between their overtones. If extra strings are ringing, the needle can jump between them. Mute the others, tune the open string to green, then unmute and match the partners.

      Safety notes

      • Protect your hearing when you give hard test blows.
      • Use a tip that fits. Slipping tips chew pins.
      • Do not yank. Broken strings and damaged pinblocks are not worth a hurried turn.

      This section is a starting guide. Professional training (for example through the Piano Technicians Guild) goes deeper. Use this app's guided steps together with careful hammer feel and firm test blows.

      Partial dial (experimental)

      This screen measures the overtones of one note. It is an experiment. It is not certified, and it is not a tuning exam. It does not replace the 88-note pre-scan.

      What to play. Tap a key, or one of the example notes. Then play that note alone and mute the other strings. The screen says this while it is listening. Example notes such as A0 through A6 are only a suggestion. Seven notes are not a rule.

      Details and the keyboard. The main screen keeps the playing instructions and a short confidence line. Tap Details for each overtone's frequency, the cents, and the stiffness fit. The keyboard shows every note from A0 to C8 in order, with the chosen note marked.

      The dial. Zero stays at the top. Sharp is to the right. Flat is to the left. The big note and the cents in the center are partial 1 when that overtone is heard. If partial 1 is not heard, the center says so and ring 1 is not drawn.

      Rings. Each ring is one overtone that was actually found, numbered 1, 2, 3, and so on. The mark on the ring is how far that overtone sits from the same number times the equal-temperament pitch of the note. Cents are a fraction of a semitone. Hertz are the measured frequency. A missing overtone is left off the dial. The app does not invent ring 1.

      Stiffness. When enough overtones are clear, the app fits a stiffness number called B. In that fit, f0 is the ideal unstiffened fundamental, not the tone you hear. Partial 1 is a different number. If the evidence is thin, B says unavailable.

      Sampling. Keep this note stores a measured note. Retry note clears it so you can try again. Save profile keeps the set on this device under a new name and includes it in Backup. Older piano profiles and the guided session are not changed. A note you did not measure is not treated as measured. Between measured notes the experimental curve may interpolate. Outside them it says unavailable.

      Custom stretch. If at least three notes have measured stiffness, including A4, the app can show an experimental 4:2 octave walk from A4. That view is not a tuning target. The guided tune does not switch to it.

      Microphone. The dial asks the browser to turn off automatic gain, noise suppression, and echo cancellation. A phone may ignore that. A short, quiet, or noisy take stays uncertain. Two notes at once, or two strings that do not match, are not drawn as one clean note.

      Interval beats are separate. The guided check that says "Comparing overtones to measure their beats" is two notes at once. This dial is one note. Intentional beats are not always zero. Nothing here claims an accuracy number for a real piano.

      Level

      • Open the Level tab. Tap Start to use the microphone. Tap Stop, leave the tab, switch apps, or play a song, and the mic turns off. Nothing is recorded or uploaded.
      • Before calibration the big number is dBFS and the screen says Not calibrated. dBFS is a digital level of the microphone signal. 0 dBFS would be a full-scale sample. It is not a sound pressure reading and it is not a safety reading.
      • The bar follows a smoothed RMS level so the number does not jump every instant. Peak hold keeps the highest smoothed RMS since you tapped Reset peak. That hold is not a true acoustic peak.
      • If the signal hits digital full scale, the status says digital clipping. Very quiet input shows Silence.
      • To estimate sound pressure, use your own reference meter. While this meter is running, type that meter's number in Reference meter reading and tap Save calibration. The app does not invent an offset. After that the big number is dB SPL est. and the notice stays on screen: the result is approximate, not A-weighted, and not a standards or safety measurement.
      • Reset calibration clears the saved offset. Calibration is stored on this device and is included in Backup. It remembers the microphone name when the browser provides one. A different mic, or a change in browser gain or processing, can make the estimate wrong. Calibrate again if that happens.
      • Start asks the browser to turn off automatic gain, noise suppression and echo cancellation. The Level tab shows what the browser actually reports. A browser may ignore the request.

      Speed and pitch

      • Pitch moves the key up or down by up to 6 semitones without changing the speed. Use the slider or the −1 and +1 buttons. Reset pitch goes back to 0.

      • When a song with saved settings plays, a small label like 75% · −2 st shows next to the time under the seek bar. Tap it to change them.

      • Speed and pitch work with the A-B loop, the Loop editor, voice cues and Auto repeat.
      • Pitch works while the app is open on screen. On iPhone, music with a pitch change may stop when the screen locks or you switch apps, and lock screen controls may not work.       Custom Clips

      • Custom Clips is a built-in playlist at the top of Playlists. It is always there and cannot be renamed or deleted.
      • To make a clip, play a song and set an A-B loop. Then tap Save Custom Clip in the Loop editor or in Loop and practice settings. Give it a name (or keep the suggested one) and tap Save.
      • A clip is a new, separate MP3 made from just the part between A and B. It is cut without changing the sound quality. Your original song is never changed. The clip keeps the song's artist and album, and gets the song's speed and pitch settings as its own.
      • Clips do not appear in your Library folders. You find them in Custom Clips and in any playlist you put them in.
      • Clips play like any other song: shuffle, repeat, A-B loop, drag to reorder, and Send.

      • In your other playlists, swipe a clip left for Add to list, Send and Remove. Remove only takes it off that playlist. If a clip is no longer in any playlist, it goes back to Custom Clips, so you never lose it.

      • Updates download by themselves when you are online. If the version number has not changed, close the app fully (swipe up from the bottom, then swipe the app away) and open it again. Sometimes it takes two tries.

      Troubleshooting

      • Voice: not-allowed. The mic is blocked. Allow it in iPhone Settings, Apps, Safari, Microphone, then tap Tap to listen.
      • Voice: service-not-allowed. Turn on Dictation in iPhone Settings, General, Keyboard.
      • Voice: no-speech. Nothing was heard. The app keeps listening. Speak a little louder or closer.
      • Voice: network. Speech needs an internet connection. Use the Again button instead.
      • Voice: audio-capture. No microphone was found, or another app is using it. Close that app and try again.
      • Voice: unsupported. This browser has no voice support. Use the Again button or Auto repeat.
      • "Tap Play to start" means the phone wants a tap before it plays. Tap Play.
      • "Could not read file, please import it again" means the app's copy of that song is damaged or missing. Import it again from Settings, Your music.
      • The update did not show. Close the app fully and open it again, maybe twice, then check the version number in Settings.
      • Songs are gone. Deleting the app icon, clearing Safari website data, or a very full iPhone can erase the app's copies. Import them again.

      iPhone limits

      • Songs must be imported into the app. It cannot read Apple Music or the Music app.
      • The microphone works only while the app is open on screen, and only when the app is opened from its secure (https) link.
      • Voice commands depend on iPhone Dictation and work best online.
      • The A-B loop and voice cues work best with the screen on and the app in front.
      • Background playback: music keeps playing when you switch apps, go to the Home Screen or lock the phone. Use the lock screen, Control Center or CarPlay to pause, skip or seek. If a call or another app pauses the music while this app is hidden, it stays paused until you come back to the app (then it picks up again) or press play on the lock screen.
      • CarPlay: the song, artist and controls show on the car screen. Navigation prompts just lower the volume for a moment. A phone call, a voice chat (for example in another app) or another app that plays sound pauses the music, and it stays paused. It starts again when you come back to the app, or press play on the car screen, the steering wheel or the lock screen. The phone does not tell the app when a call ends, so on CarPlay you may need to press play after a call. Pausing from CarPlay or the lock screen always pauses.
      • The mic cannot listen in the background. If an A-B loop reaches B while the app is hidden (for example on CarPlay), it repeats from A by itself after the gap. If it was already waiting for your voice when you left, it keeps waiting until you come back and say go, or tap Again.
      • Pitch change works while the app is open on screen. With pitch set to anything other than 0, music may stop when the screen locks or you leave the app. Speed changes do not have this limit.
      • Lock screen controls work for play, pause, next and previous.
      • Songs are stored only on this phone. Nothing is shared between devices.
